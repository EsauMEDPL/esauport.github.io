Reading 2: Your brain on ChatGPT

ChatGPT is a relatively new invention, as it only came out almost four years ago. To see the beginnings of Artificial Intelligence to today, the way AI is able to learn and evolve quickly at a fast rate is something to be considered when discussing the usage of AI. In the youtube video, "Brain Experts WARNING: Watch This Before Using ChatGPT Again! (Shocking New Discovery)" by the channel, "The Diary Of A CEO and AmenClinics", we spectate a conversation of the effects of AI to the brain and how to use AI to benefit rather than harm our brains. The main talking points were about how important it is to interact with the material given as opposed to putting in ChatGPT and inserting a command. It is hard to retell the information if there was not enough time put into understanding the material. The introduction of AI has opened many conversations recently in that the way we use AI may be harming our brains, specifically critical thinking. Critical Thinking is important to have when growing up, as the video discusses, having children just ask for the answer and not understanding how we got to the answer does not allow for interactivity with the material as the child did not think about the problem, rather it gave the hard work to the AI. This could lead to the child continuing to ask, for instance, ChatGPT for answers as it does save time that the child might want to use for something else, and it does benefit the child's grade as questions are being answered for homework. However, children might not be able to retell someone else the information they took to get that response because there was little to no time with the material for it to be embedded in the child's mind. Why does this matter? We have recently seen an increase in the usage of AI with companies. Replacing workers from many areas because it requires less money. One of the many places this discussion has appeared in is the art community as a whole. Whether the usage of AI in art is acceptable or not, is a topic many have put their own intake, as I would have disagreed. The process of creating art is something that I do cherish, the trial of errors, the time it takes, you do it all because at the end of it, you want to see a product you love. You're putting your own passion into the project or art piece being made. The AI shortens that process, it does not allow you to use your own imagination to create the visual you want. Rather it scans through the internet and tries to replicate, or make something from it, based on the prompt, depending on how descriptive it was. AI usage can be beneficial but only if you work with the material. Not allowing your brain to work does not allow the brain to retain more, nor does it allow the creative process to fully flourish.

Reading 3: Motherhood in Crisis

The story I have read and will be discussing is the story "Birth in my village" which follows the story of Memunatu and her journey of childbirth. The illustrations present in the graphic novel are very well drawn, and helps bring the tone of moment within the story. For instance, when Memunatu is facing difficulties after the unfortunate passing of her child, the illustrations often feature dark lighting. The lighting in the illustrations also are used symbolically to demonstrate the moments of the story that are very beautiful and happy, but also demonstrate the hardships and sad moments of the story. In the beginning of the story it is shown to a full moon, meaning it is nighttime and the only light source was the moon. This is also when it is said that her first child passed away and it only shows the shadows of the nurse carrying the child away. These sequences of the illustrations being in the dark helps make the images and the story, at the moment, feel more sad or down as it should. There is a panel where Memunatu is ashamed of going into the river to clean her clothes with the other women due to her losing the baby. Where Memunatu is made to look like she is in the dark, hiding away from the other women, who are seen as happy and in the light of the sun. The contrast of lighting helps visually to highlight the difference of how the women in the river are emotionally feeling and how Memunatu feels. The scene where Memunatu opens up to the woman trying to get her help or get her to the hospital to help her, the visual of the sun rising and having the sunlight hit her is telling the audience that this is the turning point of her emotions. This is presented to the panel of the lady telling Memunatu that she is not alone in this. The sun beaming on the lady, symbolizing her as perhaps hope, and Memunatu's figure has sunlight shining on her and casting away the harsh shadows in the illustration. The illustrations help provide a visual image of the story, meaning expressions are seen, the events of a story can be clearly seen, depending on what the author and illustrator wants the audience to see. In comparison to text, which uses descriptive language to help the audience imagine the story visually and left to interpretation usually. The photography that is featured in the graphic novel helps remind the audience that this story is based on someone and the artistic choices are made to emphasize certain aspects of the story. The usage of the photos also helps present the feeling of how this is how the story is ending, to make the ending end in "current time" where we see Memunatu and the baby in an emotionally happy end. It could also represent Memunatu's journey of leaving the past behind as she starts a new journey with her new child.
